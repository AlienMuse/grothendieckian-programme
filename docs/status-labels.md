# Status labels across the seven works

Every work of the Programme marks the status of each substantive claim where the claim is made, and no two of them use exactly the same set of marks. Table 3 sets the seven systems side by side, each as it is printed. The correspondences in the last column are only those the works themselves state: the sixth work says that four of its six labels are those of the whole series, in the same sense, and adds one for a known result proved again and one for a published disagreement it records without adjudicating (Series 06, Series Note); the seventh says which three of its five marks answer to the labels of the series (Series 07, Series Note). No other equation between the systems is drawn here.

The systems differ because the works need different distinctions, and the differences are informative. The third work needs a mark for evidence that may never be promoted, and one for a proved impossibility; the second needs grades for a measurable quantity and for a metaphor, which may justify nothing; the first needs a mark for a disputed claim whose controversy must remain visible. What the systems share is a rule of placement and a rule about verbs. The rule of placement is that a status belongs to the sentence where the claim is made, and that no overview, appendix, figure or table may state a claim more strongly than the chapter that establishes it; the second work adds that concealing a gap is a more serious failure than having one, and places the records of its gaps where they cannot be missed (Series 02, The reader's guarantees). In the third work's formulation, *construct*, *prove* and *establish* are used only where the object or the argument is present, and elsewhere the text says *specify*, *would imply* or *remains to be constructed* (Series 03, Preface).
